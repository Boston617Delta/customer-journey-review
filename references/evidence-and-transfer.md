# Applying research and examples within their limits

Use this when a recommendation relies on a newsletter, behavioral study, teardown, benchmark, or example from another medium. A study can suggest a useful hypothesis without establishing what will happen in the user's product.

## Trace the claim

Keep the source chain visible: original study or firsthand case → newsletter interpretation → proposed adaptation. A saved summary can support discovery; verify the relevant primary source before citing a result as established evidence. If only a teaser, abstract, or screenshot is accessible, state that limit. Do not reconstruct missing methods or invent an effect size.

Record the parts that change the decision:

- Population, product category, task, setting, and date.
- What changed and what it was compared with.
- What was measured: attitudes, stated intentions, clicks, task success, purchases, useful continued behavior, or another outcome.
- Study design and the appropriate causal confidence: experiment, observational association, practitioner experience, editorial proposal, or illustrative concept.
- Important moderators, reversals, null results, and whether the proposed mechanism was tested.

Separate reported data from the publisher's explanation and your own recommendation. Working papers, previews, and expert commentary are not automatically peer-reviewed findings. Several newsletters citing one study do not create independent replications.

## Check transfer to this decision

Compare the source and destination on audience familiarity, motivation, stakes, channel, interaction, and business model. Identify what stays relevant and what changes. A lab intention measure does not establish sales; an ad click does not establish a completed purchase; an enrollment increase does not establish useful continued behavior.

| Reference setting | Possible question to explore | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Print or outdoor ad | Can a comparison, concrete benefit, or visual explanation clarify this page? | It supplies no evidence about forms, permissions, checkout, responsiveness, or return behavior. |
| Social creative | Do the words and image communicate a coherent promise? | Overlay sizes, gaze direction, humor, and engagement findings do not prescribe app or website styling. |
| Packaging or product appearance | Would a truthful preview make the actual output easier to understand? | Physical-product perceptions do not prove an interface, service, or pricing effect. |
| Onboarding test | Does the first action connect to the promised value and a useful return path? | Starts, opens, and choices need separate downstream evaluation. |
| Pricing or promotion experiment | What comparison, timing, or redemption condition was actually tested? | A short click delay does not validate a long compulsory form or sales call. |

Label an adaptation as a hypothesis when the destination differs materially. Choose a modest, interpretable evaluation suited to the risk and volume rather than promising the source's lift.

## Resolve competing advice by the objective

Cost visibility can support informed use while also discouraging exploration. Decide whether success is effective adoption, spending control, comprehension, or another useful outcome. Make real charges and consequential limits understandable while reducing unnecessary arithmetic. Do not hide costs to increase activity.

An experiment on small coupon-redemption effort does not justify adding CAPTCHAs or extra steps to unrelated tasks. Distinguish effort that teaches, protects, or confirms a consequential choice from effort added solely to exploit commitment. Check accessibility, completion, frustration, and later outcomes.

Treat a fixed number of options, claims, reminders, stages, or days as contextual unless the relevant evidence supports it. Do not combine conflicting findings into a universal formula.

## Keep truthful communication intact

Rating visuals must preserve the actual value, scale, denominator, review count, source, and usable text equivalent where relevant. Do not round a fractional rating up visually or change a ranking's population to improve its appearance. A recommendation badge needs a truthful basis; projected popularity must remain clearly labelled as a projection with a defensible basis.

Use real, attributable experiences for testimonials and accurate comparisons for diagrams. A polished chart, precise quote, or numerical claim is not self-validating proof. Mark speculative ads and illustrative demonstrations as concepts where that affects interpretation.

For AI-assisted service, explain actual capabilities, limitations, identity, and human escalation when relevant. Natural language and humor cannot replace repair, accurate status, or accountability. Avoid trivializing serious failures or fabricating human authorship, oversight, or feelings.

## Decide what enters the method

Retain a rule only when it changes a relevant design decision, fits the requested scope, and preserves material evidence limits. Put attributed examples and research details in the approved library. Leave channel operations, viral stunts, time-bound platform advice, and unrelated sales management outside a journey review unless requested.
