# Decision checks by surface

Read the sections relevant to the current task. These are diagnostic prompts, not mandatory components or a visual template.

## Marketing pages and offers

Check whether the entry promise matches the destination, surrounding promotions, terms, and primary action. Use customer language to explain the result before introducing terminology. Make who the offer fits, its boundaries, and the consequence of the action clear.

Locate the unresolved buying uncertainty before adding proof. Match a process question to process evidence, a result claim to a scoped outcome, and a trust concern to attributable credentials or experience. A logo or general testimonial may not establish the specific claim beside it.

Use an output preview only when it helps the person understand what they receive. Identify samples, redactions, estimates, and illustrative content. Preserve necessary comparison details; a focused action does not require eliminating every useful link.

Read the headings and action labels without the body copy. They should help the person identify the offer, relevant distinctions, and next step. Pair a visual metaphor with literal information when recognition or interpretation is uncertain. A familiar brand's wordless campaign may rely on knowledge a new business cannot assume.

## Pricing, upgrades, and purchases

Check price basis, quantity or usage, billing period, renewal, included baseline, optional additions, exclusions, and consequential terms. Bring the reason for the purchase into the choice rather than forcing a return through a generic comparison.

After payment, explain what changed and whether the original task is complete. Work with external effects may require review before replaying or resuming. A payment confirmation must not imply fulfillment that has not happened.

## Intake, onboarding, and access

Ask only for information that changes the next useful action or delivery. Explain what remains, preserve input, and bring the stated goal into the resulting recommendation or artifact. A live preview should represent work that will actually be saved.

Connect permissions to a user-selected benefit. Explain scope and an appropriate fallback or consequence of refusal. Essential capabilities may be needed before a first result; optional access need not block unrelated work.

Teach unfamiliar concepts through a meaningful action when feasible. Label demonstrations accurately. Do not instruct someone to manipulate a video as though it were a working interface.

For consequential actions, consider a safe practice state before the first real action. Explain whether practice content is saved or sent and how to leave it. Require instruction only when necessary for safe, successful use; do not force a tutorial solely to accumulate commitment.

## Handoffs, progress, and client service

Distinguish an inquiry received from a booking made; a payment accepted from work started; and a delivered file from a verified outcome. Explain who acts next, where to find the result, remaining dependencies, and any genuine response or delivery expectation.

Progress should describe real completed and remaining work. Use stages when exact percentages are not defensible. Record exceptions and partial failures rather than replacing them with an unqualified success state.

For service delivery, carry the original business goal from discovery into scope, intake, deliverables, and reporting. A useful update connects completed work to evidence, uncertainty, next action, and any decision needed.

## Reminders, retention, and offboarding

Make returning easy with preserved work and a relevant resume action. Consider whether a refresher is needed without forcing unnecessary retraining. Defer optional cross-promotions when they interrupt an active goal.

After a quiz, selection, or plan, verify that the stated goal and chosen action remain easy to find on return. Ask questions that affect the experience and describe any recommendation honestly. A generic recommendation should not be presented as a uniquely personalized result.

Acknowledge meaningful completion and provide a clear stopping point. Let people continue voluntarily. A missed day need not invalidate useful work or become a reason for shaming.

Preview the actual recipient and wording of social prompts before sending. Distinguish a system-generated message from human-authored content. Respect recipient preferences and avoid duplicate reminders.

Explain cancellation, pause, renewal, data, and access consequences at the decision point. Feedback may be useful without becoming an obstruction. Confirm the actual final state and any remaining action.

## Measurement and reports

Define the metric, population, denominator, period, freshness, and uncertainty. Preserve differences between visibility, engagement, qualified demand, payment, and fulfillment. Use a verified baseline where possible and state when none exists.

Use behavior and interviews to investigate the proposed mechanism. Do not interpret a psychology label, a short-term attention spike, or a publisher's editorial score as a measured customer outcome.
