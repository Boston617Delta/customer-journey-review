# Creative research that changes a decision

Use this when a journey review involves audience research, creative concepts, campaign-to-page continuity, or reporting on creative. Keep the requested scope; a creative diagnosis is not authorization to launch ads, alter budgets, change targeting, or expand a service offer.

## Start with the business constraint

State the decision this research needs to change. Agree on the outcome and time horizon before proposing formats or gathering a large reference collection. Establish available production capacity, evidence access, and the volume of useful observations or outcomes that can realistically accumulate.

Identify whether the constraint is understanding, reach, trust, qualification, the offer, the action path, or delivery. Write a short diagnosis connecting the observed evidence to the next learning goal. If the evidence does not establish the cause, label it a hypothesis and name competing explanations.

## Ground the audience in evidence

Keep these inputs distinct:

- **Customer evidence:** interviews, reviews, support questions, observed behavior, and reconciled transactions or inquiries.
- **Creative interpretation:** who an ad or page appears to address, what it promises, and which concern it handles.
- **Business reality:** the outcome the organization needs, what it can deliver, and the constraints on production or fulfillment.

Compare the first two to find a possible gap. An apparent target audience is not a verified buyer segment. Retain source passages or observations supporting an insight and actively look for contradictory evidence. Treat frequency and expressed intensity as different qualitative signals; a vivid story is not a market-size estimate.

Describe a concrete situation, trigger, barrier, and desired result when the sources support it. Do not invent a story, infer sensitive traits, or substitute demographic stereotypes for customer evidence. A model-generated persona is a hypothesis unless grounded in observed sources.

## Separate message from execution

Distinguish the audience situation, message or benefit, angle, concept, hook, presentation format, and destination. Changing one does not require changing all of them.

Check what captures attention and how it connects to the product, proof, and next step. Audio, visuals, and text can carry different information; a striking opening is useful only when it reaches appropriate people and supports the promise that follows.

Classify the audience's current decision when useful: recognizing a problem, exploring possibilities, evaluating evidence, or committing. People can move back and forth. Use this as a retrieval aid rather than a fixed sequence everyone must complete.

For creator work, distinguish shooting style, delivery style, and the truth of the underlying experience. Conversational production does not make a fabricated testimonial authentic. Brief the goal, substantiated claims, desired information, and production constraints; preserve the performer's appropriate freedom without inventing lived experience or results.

## Build a learning plan suited to capacity

Prioritize ideas by the evidence behind them, the question they resolve, effort, and the cost of being wrong. When the cause is uncertain, explore distinct hypotheses. When a mechanism has credible support in the same context, consider a focused iteration. Do not import fixed creative mixes or output quotas from another organization.

For a brief, specify the intended audience and decision, supporting evidence, proposed change, why it should help, what stays constant where comparison matters, the useful outcome, diagnostic signals, guardrails, and the next decision. Scale detail to the production task.

Use a small number of interpretable tests when capacity is limited. A lack of delivery or exposure may be insufficient evidence, not a loss. Agree on a decision window suited to volume and conversion lag; allow inconclusive results. Keep prospecting, returning audiences, placements, and attribution definitions distinct where they change interpretation.

Organic response and competitor activity can generate ideas. Neither guarantees transfer to paid distribution, another audience, or another offer. Conflicting practitioner advice should lead to a contextual choice, not a universal rule.

## Report toward the next action

Lead with the supported finding and its implication. Present the evidence and limits, then the recommended action and owner. Keep descriptive observations separate from causal explanations.

Use the primary business outcome to assess success and secondary measures to investigate it. Compare against a relevant baseline with exposure, dates, and denominator. Do not substitute more attention, more assets, or a platform's spending preference for a customer outcome.

For service businesses and small samples, qualified conversations, direct task observation, fulfillment checks, and customer feedback can be the available evidence. State those limits rather than borrowing universal spend thresholds or manufacturing precision.
