# From a supplied reference to a useful brief

Use this for a swipe file, selected screenshot, ad, landing page, email, or creative example the user wants to learn from. Inspect the actual artifact; a filename, thumbnail, or folder label may not establish its content.

## Record what the reference can establish

Capture only the context needed to retrieve and interpret it:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Source and capture | Original URL when known, supplied file or locator, and capture/review date |
| Audience and decision | Apparent situation, barrier, and immediate task; label inferences |
| Promise and proof | What is claimed, what supports it, and which claims are unverified |
| Structure | Message sequence, visual hierarchy, hook, format, and action |
| Useful mechanism | What may help understanding or action in this context |
| Adaptation | What to borrow, what needs changing, and what to avoid |
| Evidence status | Saved for inspiration, observed behavior, publisher proposal, reported result, or measured in the relevant context |
| Next use | Candidate, selected for a brief, tested, inconclusive, or retired, when that state is actually known |

Use only the fields useful for the assignment. Preserve unknowns instead of filling them with assumptions. Several screenshots from one page are not independent performance observations.

## Adapt the reason, preserve the truth

Choose a reference because it addresses the current problem. Explain the transferable mechanism before borrowing its execution. Adjust for the user's real audience, offer, brand, channel, production capacity, and destination.

A comparison structure does not establish a competitor claim. A prominent price does not justify an invented reference price. Benefits require substantiation. A discount or reward screen requires clear conditions. A list of services or tools does not establish the user's ability or permission to offer them.

In a static screenshot, distinguish visible information from downstream behavior that was not inspected. An apparently missing term may exist elsewhere; flag what must be checked before declaring the complete flow misleading. A beautiful page, saved swipe, high view count, or long-running ad is not automatically a conversion winner.

Borrow layout logic, information order, or a presentation device without copying distinctive language, brand identity, proprietary claims, or fabricated social proof. Do not infer results or company intent from the design alone.

## Produce a brief that someone can execute

Make the relationship between evidence and design explicit:

- Intended audience situation and the decision to support.
- Observed friction or communication gap, with source context.
- The message or substantiated benefit to communicate.
- Concept and format, including the reference mechanism being adapted.
- Content sequence, necessary proof and terms, and the promised next step.
- Constraints, required assets, and information still needed.
- Evaluation: useful outcome, diagnostic measures, trust/error guardrails, and what would change the next decision.

For a family of variants, identify which change each variant explores. Do not claim a causal comparison when multiple elements or audiences change at once. Avoid generating large quantities before resolving the important uncertainty.

## Keep the library separate from the method

Private references are optional inputs, not a dependency of the public skill. Preserve access boundaries and inspect assets for account details, private messages, client information, or proprietary material before sharing. Do not automatically upload a connected Drive folder or course archive.

When publishing reusable guidance, retain the general decision rule and necessary attribution. Store actual research notes and source visuals only in the user-approved destination, with scope and evidence limits. Keep raw recordings, full transcripts, and source decks out of the skill package.
